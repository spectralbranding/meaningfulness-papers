# Unitization Before Extraction: A Pre-Registered Decomposition of Disagreement in Machine Recovery of Dependency Structure

Dmitry Zharnikov

ORCID: 0009-0000-6893-9231

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.21830220](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21830220)

Working Paper v2.1.0 – August 2026

## Abstract

Machine-extracted argument structures are increasingly read as evidence about how fields move, so measurement error at extraction propagates into whatever is built on the extracted graph. A prior pre-registered run found two machine operators did not recover the dependency structure of five argumentative documents at a declared agreement level, and located the failure upstream of relation labelling, but could not say what the disagreement was made of. This paper fixed the unit inventory before extraction, supplied it identically to both operators, and repeated every extraction five times, decomposing disagreement into selection, typing and relation drawing against a within-operator baseline. Every threshold was derived from its own reference class and deposited before data collection. Across 150 extraction calls in three unitization conditions, edge-level agreement failed its $.71$ gate in all fifteen document-by-condition cells, including the ten where units were supplied identically, and separated below the within-operator baseline in every cell ($p \leq .001$). On this corpus the predecessor's closing recommendation is therefore overturned: sharing an inventory does not rescue edge agreement, so unitization was not where the failure lived. Segmenter fidelity failed on all five documents, which bounds every fixed-inventory claim here and leaves a shared inventory of established fidelity untested.

**Keywords**: unitization, inter-annotator agreement, diagnostic replication, dependency structure, machine annotation, reproducibility, pre-registration, argument mining.

---

Argument structures recovered by machine from published text are no longer only an object of study; they are becoming an input. Extracted components and relations are assembled across documents into linked argument networks intended to be queried and analysed [@ruosch-2025-mining-inter-document-argument]. Separately, and for decades, the quantitative study of science has read *network* structure — who cites whom, who works with whom — as evidence about how fields move [@fortunato-2018-science-science]. Those two literatures are now converging: the networks in the second are beginning to be built by the extractors of the first, on argumentative rather than bibliographic relations.

That convergence is where the measurement problem enters. A network built from an extractor inherits that extractor's disagreement, and an undecomposed agreement figure cannot say whether the inherited error entered through the *nodes* or through the *edges* — which is exactly the distinction any claim about the network's shape depends on. The point generalizes an established one: a reliability coefficient does not by itself establish that data will support the use intended for them, because disagreement that is *patterned* rather than random can defeat a downstream application at agreement levels that look perfectly acceptable [@reidsma-2008-reliability-measurement-without-limits]. Node-level and edge-level disagreement are patterned differently and propagate differently, so an aggregate figure conceals precisely the structure a downstream user needs. Only a decomposed figure makes the propagated error attributable.

Zharnikov [-@zharnikov-2026bk-internalization-as-operation] pre-registered a validation of machine spine extraction and reported that it failed at its first gate: two operators from different model families did not recover the dependency structure of five argumentative documents at the declared agreement level, and they diverged on *which nodes exist* before they diverged on how nodes connect. The design could go no further, for two reasons it recorded as limitations rather than repaired. It could not **decompose** the disagreement, because unitization, node typing and relation drawing all varied at once and no quantity separates them. And it had no **noise floor**: each document was extracted once per operator, so between-operator disagreement is not distinguishable from a single operator's run-to-run variance.

This paper removes both degrees of freedom and pre-registers what follows. The unit inventory is fixed before extraction and supplied identically to both operators, so they can no longer disagree about where units begin and end; and each extraction is repeated, so an operator's agreement with itself gives the ceiling against which any between-operator number is read. It does not re-argue the internalization operation the predecessor specified, and it does not revise the predecessor's reported numbers. It inherits both and asks only what the disagreement is made of.

**Every quantity in this design is document-level.** Each coefficient is computed within one document, under one unitization condition, for one operator pair. Aggregation across the five specimens is descriptive only: no pooled coefficient is treated as an estimate of a population quantity, and no confidence statement is attached to a mean over five documents. This is stated once here and holds everywhere below.

**What this design is.** It is a **diagnostic replication** — a re-run whose declared purpose is to isolate a mechanism of failure rather than to reproduce a result [@peterson-2021-self-correction-diagnostic-integrative]. The underlying definition is the one on which a replication is a study whose *any* outcome would be diagnostic about a claim from prior research, rather than a study that repeats a procedure [@nosek-2020-what-is-replication]; the case for treating such work as ordinary rather than exceptional is made elsewhere [@zwaan-2018-making-replication-mainstream]. The distinction matters for how the corpus is read. This paper reuses all five of the predecessor's documents deliberately, and reusing an evaluation corpus is a real objection with a real literature behind it: repeated analysis of the same held-out material invalidates the guarantees that make the material informative [@dwork-2015-reusable-holdout; @dwork-2017-guilt-free-data-reuse], and standard splits acquire exactly this problem through community reuse [@gorman-2019-we-need-to-talk-standard-splits]. Under integrative criteria — does the finding replicate elsewhere — holding the corpus fixed is a weakness. Under diagnostic criteria it is the requirement: a changed corpus would confound the mechanism with the material. Naming the design is therefore not a flourish. It is what makes the reuse legible as a constraint rather than a shortcut.

**What this release contains, and what the previous one did.** Version 1.0.0 of this record contained the specification, the instruments, and the decision rule in full — every layer, what each is computed over, the reference class each threshold is derived from, **the threshold itself**, the repetition count, the chance model and its prevalence floor, the test by which the noise floor is judged to separate from the quantity it calibrates, the ordering of the conditions, and the unfavourable branches. It contained no results, and the tables that now carry them were published with their thresholds filled and their outcome cells empty. It was deposited on 2026-08-07, before any datum existed, so that thresholds preceding data is a checkable timestamp rather than an assertion. Pre-registration is common enough now to be measured as a practice rather than defended as one [@gorman-2025-prevalence-registered-reports]; what is uncommon, and what that release was for, is publishing the unfavourable branches in the same document as the design, so that the branch which overturns the predecessor's own conclusion cannot afterwards be narrated as an inconclusive run.

**This version adds the run and nothing else.** The design text below is unchanged from the deposited version: no threshold moved, no proposition was restated, and no falsifier was rewritten in the light of what it caught. The run was executed on 2026-08-08 and is reported under *Results*, with every departure from the deposited procedure named there rather than absorbed. **The unfavourable branch fired**, on every document and in every condition, which is the outcome the pre-registration existed to make reportable.
## Theory

### *What the predecessor could not separate.*

**P1.** Disagreement in machine recovery of document-scale dependency structure decomposes into three separately measurable components — unitization, node selection and typing over a fixed unit inventory, and relation drawing over a shared node set — and fixing the first by design is what makes the second and third measurable at all.

The analytic warrant is short. With units fixed and shared, node selection is a decision over a common index set, so a chance-corrected coefficient is defined over it without an alignment step. The predecessor had no shared index set and therefore no such coefficient; its node-layer statistic was computed over the nodes the two operators happened to match. Treating unitization as a separable component rather than a nuisance is licensed by the fact that unit segmentation is an established standalone task with published in-domain performance and an explicitly hard cross-domain transfer [@ajjour-2017-unit-segmentation-argumentative].

**P1 is falsifiable in a specific way.** If agreement over a fixed unit inventory cannot be computed without conditioning on a matched subset — that is, if the same conditioning the predecessor is criticised for reappears once units are fixed — the decomposition is not achieved and P1 is false.

### *The objection that the task has been replaced rather than decomposed.*

The strongest available objection to P1 comes from the literature whose instruments this design declines to use, and it is not about coefficients. It is this: *you have not shown that unitization is separable — you removed it. What is measured over a fixed inventory is a different quantity, about a different task, in which the operator no longer performs the decision whose disagreement the predecessor observed. The predecessor's failure is therefore not addressed; it is stepped around.*

**The objection is taken seriously, and the design answers it with evidence rather than with argument.** The U-free arm exists for this. It runs the *original, unmodified* task — no inventory supplied, each operator cutting its own units — in-study, on the same documents, with the same operator pair, in the same epoch. Its function is therefore not only comparability but **construct validity**. If U-free reproduces the predecessor's failure pattern while U-det does not, the intervention moved something inside a task that still behaves like the original one. If both fail in the same way, the task was not changed enough for the substitution objection to bite, and the decomposition still describes it. If U-free does *not* reproduce the predecessor's pattern, that is itself reportable and bounds every comparison this paper makes.

**The claim is correspondingly weak, and is stated at that width.** This design does not assert that the fixed-index quantity *is* the free-unitization quantity; C2 says the two are not comparable by magnitude, and that holds. What it asserts is narrower: the two are measured on the same documents, by the same operators, in the same epoch, so what changes between them is attributable to the unitization condition and to nothing else about the study.

### *How the decomposition relates to the published composite.*

Segmentation and structure agreement have been composed into a single score in argument mining for a decade [@duthie-2016-cass-technique-evaluating], and a unified coefficient scores unitizing and categorisation jointly under a principled alignment [@mathet-2015-unified-holistic-gamma]. The relation between those instruments and this one has to be argued rather than merely asserted, because a reader who knows them will ask why a composite does not already answer the question.

A composite treats unitization as a **nuisance parameter**: it is absorbed into one number so that two systems can be ranked, and the ranking is valid precisely because the absorption is principled. The claim here is stronger. It is that unitization is **separable** — that with the inventory held fixed and shared, the residual disagreement is a quantity in its own right, defined over a common index set, rather than the remainder left after a nuisance is integrated out. Separability is what converts a nuisance parameter into a measured component, and it is the extension a composite does not provide. The two instruments answer different questions: the composite answers *how much* two operators agree, which is what ranking needs; the decomposition answers *where* they disagree, which is what repair needs. This paper reports both, and where they diverge the divergence is reported rather than resolved in the decomposition's favour.

### *The noise floor.*

**P2.** A between-operator agreement number is uninterpretable without a within-operator baseline. Machine operators are unreliable against themselves across repeated runs, so low between-operator agreement may be variance rather than disagreement, and high agreement may be a shared prior rather than a recovered structure.

Self-inconsistency in machine judges across repeated runs is reported rather than hypothesised [@haldar-2025-rating-roulette-selfinconsistency], and the human comparison is not a ceiling either: intra-annotator stability over a two-week interval is well short of perfect on every task measured [@abercrombie-2025-consistency-is-key]. The predecessor has no within-operator baseline, which it recorded as a limitation rather than repairing, precisely because repairing it after the result would have been a protocol change made in light of an outcome.

**The baseline measures reproducibility of the instrument as deployed, not the model's epistemic uncertainty.** Hosted endpoints are not stationary: provider-side dynamic batching makes temperature-zero decoding nondeterministic [@he-2025-defeating-nondeterminism-llm-inference], and silent updates drift a model's behaviour across any window that is not tight [@chen-2024-chatgpt-behavior-changing]. Both are components of what a replicator faces, so they belong inside the quantity rather than being idealised away. That framing is not invented here: reproducibility in this field has an explicit metrological treatment, in which it is measurement precision on a continuum rather than binary success [@belz-2022-metrological-perspective-reproducibility], with a quantified assessment method [@belz-2022-quantified-reproducibility-assessment; @belz-2026-quantified-reproducibility-four-types] and a field-level finding that experiments are frequently not repeatable at all [@belz-2023-non-repeatable-experiments].

**The gap this occupies is narrower than it first appears, and is stated at its true width.** Reproducibility of machine annotation is studied and has a method; machine annotators are routinely evaluated against human gold standards [@ziems-2024-can-large-language-models], and whether a machine annotator is statistically indistinguishable from a human one has been put as an equivalence question [@he-2025-can-we-hide-machines-crowd]. What remains genuinely absent is narrower: inter-system agreement between two *generative* operators on a *structured extraction* task, reported as a reliability coefficient with **no gold standard** and no consensus reference.

**P2's falsifier covers the case that will actually occur.** The baseline is uninformative, and P2's practical force void, in either of two events: within-operator agreement at or near ceiling on every document, or — the likelier one — within-operator agreement statistically indistinguishable from between-operator agreement on every document, since a floor that does not separate from the quantity it calibrates cannot calibrate it.

**The test is fixed here, so the falsifier can fire mechanically.** The comparison is between two dependent agreement coefficients computed on the same documents, which is not a comparison to improvise once the coefficients are in hand. Per document, the difference between the within-operator and the between-operator coefficient is referred to a **paired permutation null** under which the within-pair and between-pair labellings of the repeated extractions are exchangeable, with 10,000 permutations and a percentile bootstrap interval on the difference at $B = 10{,}000$. The stream is offset by a stable digest of the document identifier rather than by a process-dependent hash, so the null regenerates byte-identically from the committed record. The level is $.05$ per document. **P2's falsifier fires only when the interval includes zero on every document**; a mixed outcome is reported per document and does not fire it. Either outcome is a result about this class of operator and is reported as one.

### *What the predecessor's node layer was worth.*

**P3.** The predecessor's node-layer statistic is favourably biased by conditioning on matched nodes, and the size of the bias is measurable by recomputing that layer under a coefficient that charges for boundary disagreement rather than excluding it.

**The direction is declared now, before the recomputation: the expectation is that the predecessor's node layer looks worse.** Its node-boundary precision runs far below its node-type agreement, so the excluded mass is large, and a coefficient that charges for that mass cannot leave the statistic unchanged unless the excluded units happen to be typed as the matched ones were. If the recomputation leaves the layer materially unchanged, the conditioning was not doing the work attributed to it and P3 is false. The result is reported whichever way it comes out.

**P3 is reported as a robustness check, and the demotion is deliberate.** Three co-equal contributions inside one pre-registration is one too many, and a re-analysis of one's own predecessor will be read as a secondary check however it is placed — so it is placed there on purpose. The demotion is structural only: the recomputation and its table sit in Appendix A, while the declared unfavourable direction stays here, in the main text. Burying the direction would forfeit the only thing the re-analysis buys, which is the visible discipline of correcting oneself against one's own interest — a practice with a published precedent in which authors report the loss of confidence in their own prior findings [@rohrer-2021-putting-self-in-self-correction].

### *The claim under test.*

**P4.** The located failure is testable and may be refuted. If edge-level agreement still fails with the units fixed and the noise floor known, then unitization was not where the failure lived, and the predecessor's own conclusion is overturned by its declared successor.

P4 is the falsification instrument rather than a claim to defend. On what the predecessor found, the unfavourable branch is the likely one, which is why the primary contribution is the decomposition and not the verdict. Stating P4 this way pre-commits the unfavourable branch; reporting an inconclusive run instead of it is the specific failure mode the pre-declaration exists to prevent.

### *Boundary conditions.*

**C1 — a recoverable argument.** The artifact carries an argument with a recoverable dependency structure. Inherited unchanged; data releases, catalogues and purely narrative work stay out of scope.

**C2 — quantities travel with their inventory.** In the intervention conditions a unit inventory is fixed before any extraction and supplied identically to both operators. Every quantity is relative to the inventory it was computed under, and quantities from different inventories — including the free condition, which has none — are **not** compared by magnitude. Unitizing coefficients and fixed-index classification coefficients occupy different probability spaces [@artstein-poesio-2008-inter-coder-agreement; @krippendorff-2016-reliability-unitizing-continua], so the free and fixed conditions are contrasted by what each makes measurable, never by whose coefficient is larger.

**C3 — the corpus is held fixed.** The specimen corpus is the predecessor's five documents, unchanged. This design measures the effect of the unitization condition, not the effect of a corpus, and a changed corpus would confound the two.

**C4 — two ways this can produce an uninterpretable result.** First, if node selection is near-universal over the fixed inventory, node-selection agreement is high by construction and carries no information; a base-rate-aware chance model and a declared floor are therefore required before the run rather than after it. Second, if the unitizer's own error is large on a document, the operators inherit it identically and the measured agreement describes the unitizer rather than the operators. Unit segmentation is hard across domains, so this is a live condition and not a formality.

**C5 — the framing has a limit, and it is declared.** The diagnostic-replication frame licenses the corpus reuse because the object of study is a mechanism. The distinction the frame turns on is an inherited one: a study whose any outcome is diagnostic about a prior claim is a replication, whereas a study asking whether the claim survives a changed setting is a *generalizability test* [@nosek-2020-what-is-replication]. This design is the first and not the second. That licence lapses the moment the paper is read as estimating *how often* extraction fails, or how well it would work on other documents. Under that reading the reuse is a defect rather than a requirement, and this design supports no answer at all. The claim is a controlled contrast on a fixed corpus; where a claim about frequency, coverage or transfer is wanted, this design is out of scope rather than weakly informative.

**Two assumptions are carried without test, and their direction is declared.** Two arms drawn from different model families are an *operational* separation, not a statistical one: nothing guarantees uncorrelated errors, and where errors correlate positively agreement is easier to obtain — which makes a pass weak and a failure conservative. And the operators are machines, so this design measures an instantiation of the procedure rather than the privileged one.

## Method

### *Three conditions, ordered in advance.*

**M1.** Three conditions run on the same five documents, with the same operator pair, in the same epoch. **U-free** supplies no inventory and is the free-unitization condition; it carries three functions — the comparability control, the control for granularity mismatch (L6), and the construct-validity evidence that the fixed-inventory arms have not simply replaced the task. **U-det** applies a deterministic published segmentation rule with no model in the loop, and is the **primary intervention**. **U-mod** takes units from a model family used neither as operator nor as rater, and is the realistic deployment condition. The ordering is fixed here so that a result cannot later be reported from whichever condition ran the better way.

**Running U-free in-study is what makes the contrast sound.** The alternative — comparing this study's fixed-index coefficients against the predecessor's free-unitization numbers — was considered and rejected, because the two occupy different probability spaces and a cross-study magnitude comparison would not have been valid. Running the free condition inside this study puts the contrast inside one study, one operator pair and one epoch.

### *Validating the segmenter before using it.*

**M1a.** A segmenter whose own reliability is unknown cannot serve as a control condition, because its error passes silently to the operators and is scored as theirs. The deterministic rule is fixed by version; its own reported reliability is stated; and its treatment of displayed mathematics is declared before the run as an explicit rule rather than handled case by case, since a rule that splits on sentence punctuation shatters displayed equations into fragments and one of the five specimens is a mathematical exposition.

**The segmenter is also validated on this corpus, before any operator call.** A published accuracy figure is measured on the segmenter's own benchmarks, and a rule that scores well on newswire need not score well on a mathematical exposition or a philosophical essay. Its agreement with a hand-adjudicated inventory on these five documents is therefore established first and reported per document beside the operator quantities. **The adjudicated inventory is itself an instrument and is produced as one**: two adjudicators segment each document independently under the declared rule, blind to the study's hypotheses and to which condition the inventory will serve; a third resolves disagreements; and the adjudicators' own agreement is reported beside the segmenter's. Without that, Layer S compares the instrument against its own designer, and the degree of freedom L3 closes reopens one level up. The **order** is the substantive part: measured afterwards, the segmenter's error becomes available as an explanation for whatever the operators did, which is precisely the researcher degree of freedom L3 names.

### *Why the deterministic rule is a sentence segmenter.*

**M1b.** The affirmative case for imposing a rule at all is established rather than assumed: human subjects segment discourse *reliably* once a segmentation criterion is declared, which is the finding on which a declared criterion is an improvement over unconstrained judgement rather than a straitjacket imposed on it [@passonneau-1993-intention-based-segmentation; @passonneau-1997-discourse-segmentation-human-automated]. U-det uses a rule-based sentence segmenter installable at a fixed release, permissively licensed, reporting its own accuracy against a published rule set — $97.92\%$ on the English Golden Rule Set and $97.00\%$ on a biomedical corpus [@sadvilkar-2020-pysbd-pragmatic-sentence-boundary]. The coarser granularity this implies is declared rather than hidden, and the two finer discourse-unit rules are rejected for stated reasons rather than passed over.

The first finer rule defines units on syntactic clause boundaries and reports the strongest blind segmentation reliability available for any rule — $\kappa = .874$ rising to $\kappa = .972$ on previously unseen documents [@carlson-2001-building-discourse-tagged-corpus]. Its gold corpus is licensed, so adopting the rule would mean reimplementing it with no evidence that the reimplementation is faithful. The second is distributed only as a single untagged archive requiring an interpreter and library generation two decades old, and it bundles a third-party script carrying no licence, so it cannot be published as reproduction code [@tofiloski-2009-syntactic-lexical-discourse-segmenter]. The two rules also disagree with each other — the second deliberately does not treat complements of attributive verbs as units, and one under-segments where the other over-segments — so there is no single *the* discourse rule to inherit.

**The consequence is a limitation, not a finding.** Sentence units are coarser than discourse units, so U-det tests a coarser shared inventory than the finest one available, and the granularity-mismatch threat is argued at that granularity. Under-segmenting and over-segmenting rules fail in opposite directions, which is why the choice is declared here rather than defaulted.

**The reply this invites is worth stating in full, because it is the sharpest one available to the segmentation community.** It is that this design has not removed a segmentation-agreement problem so much as converted it into a segmentation-*choice* problem, and that the choice of sentence granularity over discourse granularity is itself the study's most consequential finding rather than a preliminary. That reply is accepted, not deflected. It is why the choice is argued here on stated grounds, why U-mod runs a second, model-produced inventory rather than leaving one rule unexamined, and why the effect of granularity is reported per document rather than assumed uniform. What the design does not do is settle which granularity is correct; it fixes one, declares it, and measures what follows.

### *The mathematical unit rule.*

**M1c.** No published segmentation rule for mathematical text was located, so this rule is declared and defended as this paper's own instrument decision. **A displayed expression attaches to the sentence that introduces it and is not a unit of its own, and no unit boundary is placed inside symbolic material.**

The grounding is the established result that the textual and the symbolic in rigorous mathematical prose are grammatically intertwined, and that ambiguity in the symbolic resolves only once the types of the entities are known [@ganesalingam-2013-language-of-mathematics; @aberdein-2017-review-language-of-mathematics]. It follows that a rule severing symbolic material from its sentence destroys the unit rather than dividing it. **What is not claimed, because no source supports it:** that displayed and inline mathematics constitute different unit *types*. That distinction was carried through an earlier design pass and is dropped. The rule's effect on the mathematical specimen's inventory size is reported beside the other four documents.

### *The operator task.*

**M2.** The operator task is narrowed to three decisions over the given units: which units are nodes, what type each node carries, and which typed edges hold between nodes. **An operator may not re-cut a unit.** That single restriction is the whole intervention.

### *Repetition, and what it buys.*

**M3.** Every extraction is repeated $k$ times per operator, per document, per condition, under identical parameters, with $k$ fixed before the run for statistical reasons and not budgetary ones. There is no principled published guidance for $k$; the practical constraint is that $k = 3$ is the bare minimum for a repeated-measures reliability statistic and $k \geq 5$ is needed to stabilise a variance estimate. **$k = 5$** — the floor of that argued range and not above it, because nothing in the reference class supports a larger number and a $k$ chosen for comfort rather than for a stated reason is a budget decision wearing a statistical costume. The arithmetic is published so the count is auditable: 3 conditions $\times$ 5 documents $\times$ 2 operators $\times$ 5 repetitions $=$ 150 extraction calls. All repetitions for a document run in as tight a window as rate limits permit, and model version and epoch are logged per call, because provider-side updates drift across a long window. **Every between-operator quantity is reported beside its within-operator counterpart, computed on the same statistic.**

For the continuous repeated-measures component the field convention is imported rather than reinvented, and the import is stated as one. Two notational systems are in use [@shrout-1979-intraclass-correlations-rater-reliability; @mcgraw-1996-forming-inferences-intraclass-correlation], and the quantity this design needs is **absolute agreement** rather than consistency: a systematic offset between runs is a reproducibility failure, not something to be normalised away. The runs are interchangeable repetitions rather than fixed named conditions, and each reported reading comes from a single run rather than a mean of $k$. That gives $\text{ICC}(A,1)$, and the reporting format follows the published selection guideline [@koo-2016-guideline-selecting-reporting-icc]. Intraclass correlation is the convention in psychometrics and clinical measurement rather than in computational linguistics, so it is presented as a deliberate import with a stated reason.

### *Scoring the nested decision.*

**M4.** The nested decision is scored in **hierarchical** form rather than flattened into a joint label space: one coefficient for the binary include-or-exclude decision over the shared inventory, and a **conditional** coefficient for typing computed over the units both operators included. Flattening conflates the base rate of inclusion with the accuracy of typing, which would dissolve the decomposition this paper contributes.

**The principle is adopted, not invented, and so is the conditioning rule.** A peer-reviewed treatment of two-stage annotation in learner corpora establishes that when a decision is locate-then-describe, the two stages' agreement must be reported *separately* rather than as one measurement [@dobric-2023-identifying-errors-learner-corpus]. That treatment goes further than the principle, and in exactly the direction this design needs: it states that agreement on the second stage has agreement on the first as a *necessary precondition*, and that only the positions at least two annotators marked are taken into consideration when scoring the second stage — positions no one marked are not locations at all and cannot be scored. **The conditioning rule used here is therefore that published protocol applied to a two-operator design**, where "at least two annotators agreeing" and "both operators agreeing" coincide. What remains this paper's own is the transfer to select-then-type over a fixed inventory and the chance model under it, neither of which the source supplies. **Two near-relatives are named rather than claimed as sources**: coefficients for the case where categories are not defined in advance address a different problem, that of observers generating their own categories [@brennan-1974-measuring-agreement-categories-undefined; @brook-1984-agreement-categories-not-specified]; and conditional kappa conditions on the *category* assigned rather than on a prior *stage* [@light-1971-measures-response-agreement; @kvalseth-1985-weighted-conditional-kappa]. A recent generalisation to hierarchical *categories* is likewise a different object from a hierarchical *decision* [@moons-2025-generalization-fleiss-kappa-hierarchical]. The related problem of scoring agreement where the locations themselves are not prespecified is treated in the free-response literature [@carpentier-2017-kappa-free-response-assessments; @shih-2018-problems-kappa-lesion-detection], which is the situation the fixed inventory is designed to escape.

**Edge recovery is reported twice**: as triple overlap over the agreed node set [@cai-knight-2013-smatch-evaluation-metric], and over the full fixed inventory — the second being what the predecessor could not compute, and the one that does not condition disagreement away.

**The composite score is secondary in inferential status and co-equal in reporting prominence, and the asymmetry is deliberate.** It is secondary because the decomposition is the contribution: a single number that ranks two systems cannot answer where they disagree, which is what this design exists to measure, so the composite does not gate and no conclusion rests on it alone. It is co-equal in reporting because burying it would earn the fair reply that the standard instrument was avoided rather than answered — it is therefore computed for every document and every condition, printed beside the layered form, and **any divergence between the two is reported as a finding rather than resolved in the decomposition's favour**. Declaring the status now rather than after the run is what stops a favourable composite from being promoted, or an unfavourable one from being demoted, once both are visible.

### *Thresholds are re-derived, not inherited.*

**M4a.** Each layer's threshold is argued in this pre-registration with its reference class named, and the predecessor's gate is **not** inherited. Selection over a shared inventory has its own published track record and it is poor. Human agreement on extractive sentence selection runs at $\kappa = .127$ to $\kappa = .274$ across summary lengths [@radev-2003-evaluation-challenges-document-summarization]; strict agreement on selection from online debate sits at $\kappa = .28$ and $\alpha = .27$ in the authors' deposited version [@sanchan-2018-gold-standard-online-debates], whose relaxed figures are internally inconsistent and are not used here; the upper end of the reference class, on binary salience over segmented reviews, reaches $\kappa = .51$ [@angelidis-2018-summarizing-opinions-aspect-extraction]. Carrying the predecessor's gate to the node layer would declare a floor no human reference pool reaches. What raises coder agreement is itself an empirical question with a meta-analytic answer, which is the reason a threshold is argued from a reference class rather than from convention [@bayerl-2011-what-determines-intercoder-agreement].

The layers with their own reference classes are stated with them. Relation-type agreement over *fixed* units in scientific discourse runs $\kappa = .699$ to $\kappa = .772$ across annotator pairs [@yang-2018-scidtb-discourse-dependency], which is the closest published analogue to this design's typing layer. The ceiling for graph annotation commensurable with a gold-free operator-versus-operator design is annotator-versus-annotator agreement of $.71$ [@banarescu-2013-abstract-meaning-representation], not the higher annotator-versus-consensus figure; quoting the latter would compare a gold-referenced quantity against a gold-free one, which is the same structural error this paper identifies elsewhere. Multi-sentence extensions of the same metric are available where document-scale scoring is needed [@naseem-2022-docamr-multisentence-representation]. That an inherited evaluation metric can systematically overstate performance is not speculative: it has been demonstrated in detail for parser evaluation [@morey-2018-dependency-perspective-rst-parsing], which is the methodological precedent for re-deriving rather than adopting.

Each of the five thresholds follows, tied to the reference class beside it rather than to judgement.

**Layer S, segmenter fidelity: $\kappa \geq .80$** against the adjudicated inventory. The human blind segmentation floor is $.874$. This gate sits deliberately below it, for two reasons: the comparison here is a deterministic rule against human adjudication, which is harder than human against human; and a rule agreeing below $.80$ is describing itself rather than the document, which is C4's second negative-scope condition.

**Layer 1, node selection: $\kappa \geq .51$.** The reference class is not the broad summarisation range but the single published figure computed over the *same decision structure* — binary salience over pre-segmented units [@angelidis-2018-summarizing-opinions-aspect-extraction]. The lower human figures are a different decision, made over unsegmented text where the annotator must find the unit as well as judge it, and they are reported for context rather than used as the gate.

**Layer 2, conditional typing: $\kappa \geq .70$** — the lower bound of the published range for relation and type agreement over *fixed* units in scientific discourse [@yang-2018-scidtb-discourse-dependency]. A machine pair claiming to type over a shared inventory should meet the weakest human pair reported on that task.

**Layer 3, edge recovery over agreed nodes: $\geq .71$**, the annotator-versus-annotator figure for gold-free graph annotation [@banarescu-2013-abstract-meaning-representation]. Two features of that figure make it the right one to use. It is annotator-versus-annotator rather than annotator-versus-consensus — the consensus-referenced figures in the same source, $.83$ and $.79$, presuppose a reference this design does not have. And the pair that produced it were *newly trained* rather than the expert pool, which is nearer to an untrained machine pair than an expert ceiling would be. **No discount is applied for document scale**, although the published figure comes from a fixed scheme on sentence-scale semantics. **The task is not the same task**, and that is a comparability limitation rather than a defect in the figure: the source annotates sentence-level semantic structure, this design recovers document-scale dependency structure, and the figure is imported on the argument that both are gold-free structured annotation scored by triple overlap. Discounting it would require a number for how much harder document scale is, and no such number exists to derive one from.

**Because that number is imported from an easier task, Layer 3 carries two criteria rather than one, and the second is the informative one.** The *absolute* criterion is the $.71$ ceiling above. It is a ceiling borrowed from a fixed scheme and a trained pool, so clearing it would be a strong result and failing it is the expected one — which means the absolute criterion alone carries little information in the branch the design considers likely. The *relative* criterion is therefore declared beside it: **between-operator edge agreement is read against the within-operator baseline on the same statistic, using the separation test of P2**. Two operators that agree with each other about as well as each agrees with itself have recovered whatever structure this instrument can recover, whether or not that level clears a ceiling set on sentence-scale semantics by trained humans. Reporting both keeps the published benchmark honest and keeps the layer informative under either outcome. Neither criterion is allowed to substitute for the other after the fact: both are reported for every document and condition. A discount would be derived from an expectation about this study's outcome rather than from evidence, which is the move this paper forbids elsewhere. The consequence is a hard gate, and a hard gate here is conservative: it makes a pass strong, and the decomposition is the contribution whether or not the verdict is favourable.

**Layer 4, edge recovery over the full inventory: no independent threshold; its *ratio* to Layer 3 must exceed $.70$.** This layer has no published reference class, so setting a level for it would be inventing a number rather than deriving one. What the ratio measures is the worth of the conditioning this paper criticises — how much of the agreed-node figure survives once the nodes the operators did not share are charged rather than excluded. Gating the ratio rather than the level ties the layer to the methodological complaint it exists to instrument, and it is the one threshold here that is constructed rather than inherited from a reference class.

**The sensitivity table is published with its thresholds filled and its outcome cells empty, before any data exist** (Table 2).

### *Reporting the full marginal picture.*

**M4b.** Every chance-corrected coefficient is reported with its marginal distributions, a prevalence index and raw agreement. The paradox is old and well documented — high raw agreement with a low coefficient, driven by skewed marginals [@feinstein-1990-high-agreement-low-kappa-i; @cicchetti-1990-high-agreement-low-kappa-ii] — and the recommendation to report the marginals, a prevalence index and raw agreement alongside the coefficient comes from the same literature [@byrt-1993-bias-prevalence-kappa], with worked demonstrations [@zec-2017-high-agreement-high-prevalence; @shankar-2014-observer-agreement-paradoxes] and a practical selection guide [@zapf-2016-measuring-interrater-reliability-nominal].

**Prevalence-robust coefficients are declined as an escape, and the reason is structural rather than simulated.** The alternative coefficient's chance model does not condition on the observed marginals, so it tends toward $1$ as prevalence tends toward $0$ or $1$ [@gwet-2008-computing-interrater-reliability], which rewards an operator for assigning the dominant label — the exact failure mode this design exists to exclude. The independent comparison establishes that it is not a substitute: it rises as prevalence departs from even at fixed raw agreement, it is nonzero under independence, and the conventional verbal bands do not transfer to it [@vach-2023-gwets-ac1-not-substitute]. That last point is a published instance of the threshold-inheritance error M4a addresses. The most-cited comparison in its favour has the coefficient's own author among its authors and is not independent evaluation [@wongpakaran-2013-comparison-cohens-kappa-ac1]. The position that the paradoxes are the coefficient behaving correctly, rather than defects to be engineered away, is stated in the same literature [@krippendorff-2013-dissenting-view-paradoxes]. If a prevalence-robust coefficient is reported at all it is reported beside the standard one with that objection stated.

**No published guidance pre-specifies a prevalence floor**, so the floor this design needs is constructed and defended here rather than cited. It is the highest-value item the pre-registration fixes, and it is fixed in three parts.

**The chance model is the observed-marginal one, kept rather than replaced.** Chance agreement is derived from each operator's own observed marginals, and the depression this produces under skewed base rates is treated as the coefficient reporting correctly, not as a defect to engineer away — which is the position argued above and the reason a prevalence-robust substitute is declined.

**The floor is an interpretability gate, not a correction.** The prevalence index is reported beside every coefficient, and **where that index exceeds $.85$ on a document, that document's chance-corrected coefficient is reported but does not gate**. It is declared uninterpretable in advance, and the document's evidence rests on the marginals, raw agreement and $F_1$ instead. This instantiates C4's first negative-scope condition: selection that is near-universal over the inventory carries no information, however high the raw agreement.

**The base rate is predicted before the run, and that is what makes the floor a pre-registration rather than an escape hatch.** The expected selection base rate for each document is the predecessor's recorded node count divided by this study's inventory size for that document. Both quantities exist before any operator is called — the node counts are in the predecessor's published record, and the inventory sizes are computed by the segmenter. The predicted base rate and the predicted prevalence index are therefore **published per document before the run** (Table A2), and any document whose predicted index already breaches $.85$ is declared non-gating *in advance* rather than after its coefficient has been seen. A floor announced after the coefficients are in hand would be indistinguishable from an excuse; a floor whose triggering documents are named in advance is a commitment.

One published result bears on the reporting choice: chance-corrected measures converge on the F-measure as the number of negative instances grows [@hripcsak-2005-agreement-fmeasure-reliability]. For a design in which the great majority of candidate units are rejected, that is a principled basis for reporting $F_1$ beside the chance-corrected coefficient rather than appealing to a contested alternative coefficient. The general warning against reading any single reliability number without its context is taken as read [@reidsma-2008-reliability-measurement-without-limits].

### *The published joint coefficient, computed rather than argued against.*

**M5a.** A published unified coefficient scores unitizing and categorisation jointly under a principled alignment [@mathet-2015-unified-holistic-gamma], with a maintained, version-pinnable implementation [@titeux-2021-pygamma-agreement], and it has a **companion** that isolates categorisation agreement while setting positional discrepancy aside [@mathet-2017-agreement-measure-gamma-cat]. The companion is the near-neighbour of this design's conditional typing statistic, and it is the harder of the two to dispose of; silence about it would be the most visible hole in the contribution. The family's origin and current form are the unitizing-reliability coefficients [@krippendorff-1995-reliability-unitizing-continuous; @krippendorff-2016-reliability-unitizing-continua], which have been applied to discourse segmentation directly [@pons-borderia-2021-interannotator-agreement-spoken].

**Neither member is argued against a priori. Both are computed in-study, beside the decomposition.** The method states what the conditional typing statistic does that the companion does not, and the honest candidate distinction is this: the companion sets position aside as a nuisance, whereas this design *removes* position by construction and then measures selection as a quantity in its own right. **If that distinction does not survive contact with the companion's definition, the paper says so, and the decomposition's novelty narrows to the selection stage.** Where any two of the three instruments diverge, the divergence is reported rather than resolved in the decomposition's favour.

### *The declared re-analysis.*

**M5.** The predecessor's node layer is recomputed under a joint unitizing coefficient as a declared re-analysis whose unfavourable direction is stated in advance (P3). **The predecessor's frozen guidelines are not edited and its reported numbers are not revised.** The re-analysis is additive and is labelled as this paper's, not as a correction to the predecessor's record. That the guidelines stay frozen has a cost worth naming: using measured agreement to improve annotation guidelines is a recognised and productive practice in argument annotation [@teruel-2018-increasing-argument-annotation-reproducibility], and this design forgoes it. It does so because guidelines revised in light of this study's own agreement figures would no longer be the instrument the predecessor used, and the contrast would be lost.

### *What each layer decides.*

The rule below is fixed before any data collection. Table 1 states the layers, what each is computed over, and its reference class; Table 2 states each threshold, the derivation behind it, and — in advance — how the conclusion would change at the neighbouring value in either direction, so that a reported outcome cannot be read as the only outcome the rule would have permitted. No threshold in Table 2 moves after this release.

Layer B carries no threshold: it calibrates the layers above rather than gating them, and P2's separation test is what it is read through.

### *The branch that overturns the predecessor.*

**If edge-level agreement still fails with the units fixed and the noise floor known, unitization was not where the failure lived.** That branch refutes the predecessor's closing recommendation, and it is pre-committed here rather than left available to be narrated afterwards as an inconclusive run. On what the predecessor found, it is the likely branch, which is why the decomposition rather than the verdict is the primary contribution: the decomposition is informative under either outcome, and the verdict is informative only under one.

**Power calculations are excluded, deliberately.** At five documents this design is under-powered for generalization by construction, and it makes no generalization claim — so a power statement would answer a question the design does not ask and would imply one it does not make. What replaces it is the sensitivity table and the explicit document-level reporting.

## Threats This Design Carries

### *The mirror image of the predecessor's error.*

**L4 — fixing the units may manufacture agreement rather than measure it.** If node selection is near-universal over sentence-like units, the coefficient is high by construction. The predecessor conditioned disagreement away by scoring only matched nodes; this design could define it away by fixing units so fine that selection is nearly unanimous. This is named here as the **likeliest** way this paper fools itself. It is not the most damaging — that is L6 below, and the two are separated deliberately, because likelihood and severity are different axes and a threat register that conflates them mitigates the wrong one first. The mitigation is structural: a base-rate-aware chance model and a declared floor, both fixed before the run, plus the full marginal report of M4b.

**L6 — granularity mismatch.** Fixing the units does not eliminate the unitization problem so much as trade it. If the imposed rule cuts at a grain that conflicts with an operator's internal representation of the argument, the operator will fail to draw edges, and the failure will be recorded as an edge-drawing deficit when it is an artifact of the segmentation. This is less likely than L4 but **more damaging**, because it produces a result that looks exactly like the one the paper is testing for and would therefore be believed, and displayed mathematics is where it bites hardest. The trade-off between granularity and reliability is documented rather than speculative [@crible-2019-reliability-vs-granularity], and segmentation-evaluation metrics are themselves sensitive to how near-misses are charged [@pevzner-2002-critique-improvement-segmentation-metric; @fournier-2013-evaluating-text-segmentation-boundary; @scaiano-2012-getting-more-from-segmentation]. **The U-free arm is the control**: if U-det fails and U-free fails in the same way, the segmentation did not produce the failure.

### *Threats carried forward and not repaired.*

**L1 — $N$ is five documents**, and the corpus is deliberately unchanged. No generalization claim is available; the paper claims a controlled contrast.

**L2 — training-data contamination on the philosophical specimen** is unchanged from the predecessor. It inflates a positive result specifically and cannot be removed by blinding.

**L3 — the unitizer is a new researcher degree of freedom.** A segmenter imported across domains carries its own error, and this design must not launder that error into the operators' scores. Both intervention conditions are declared in advance with the primary fixed, the segmenter is validated on this corpus before any operator call (M1a), and the unitizer's output is published with the record so a reader can inspect what the operators were given.

**Two arms are declared out of scope for this round, with the reason and the successor named.** A **human operator arm** would test what the predecessor conceded and nothing has examined — whether human operators clear the bar these machines are being read against. It is excluded here because it changes the object: this design measures the *decomposition* of machine-versus-machine disagreement, and adding a human reference turns it into a comparison, one that five documents could not support even if it were wanted. The gap this paper occupies is gold-free inter-system agreement precisely because no human reference is available in it. A **local fixed-batch control arm** would isolate the model's own contribution to run-to-run variance from provider-side batching, and it is excluded for a different reason — it would change the operator class from hosted models to open-weight local ones, which is a different instrument answering a different question (see L7). Both are named as successors rather than as omissions, and neither is a limitation of this design so much as a boundary of it.

**L7 — the within-operator baseline includes provider-side variance that is not a property of the model.** This is addressed by definition rather than by repair: the quantity is reproducibility of the instrument as deployed, which is what a replicator faces. A local fixed-batch control arm would isolate the model's own contribution, but it would change the operator class from hosted models to open-weight local ones, so it is recorded as an explicit scope decision rather than assumed in.

**L5 — the sequencing constraint, now discharged.** This paper cites its predecessor and could not be deposited before it. The predecessor was deposited on 2026-08-07 under a resolving concept DOI, so the ordering is satisfied rather than waived. The constraint is recorded rather than deleted, because it still binds every later round of this arc and because the record should show that it was declared before it was met.

## Results

### *What the run was.*

The run executed on 2026-08-08 under protocol version `2026bl/1.0.0` with prompts at version `1.0.0`, study seed 20260808, and $k = 5$. The published arithmetic was met exactly: 3 conditions $\times$ 5 documents $\times$ 2 operators $\times$ 5 repetitions $=$ 150 extraction calls, with no hard failure and no missing cell. Every between-operator quantity below is the mean over the 25 cross-operator repetition pairs for its cell, and every within-operator baseline the mean over the 20 same-operator pairs, computed on the same statistic (M3).

**Every quantity is document-level, as declared.** Aggregation across the five specimens is descriptive only, no pooled coefficient is treated as an estimate of a population quantity, and no confidence statement is attached to a mean over five documents. Ranges given below are ranges over cells, not intervals.

### *Segmenter fidelity, established first, and failing on all five documents.*

Layer S was computed before any operator call, as M1a requires; the ordering is a property of the committed record rather than an assertion. **It fails the $\kappa \geq .80$ gate on every document**: $\kappa = .169$ (VC1), $-.005$ (VC2), $.250$ (VC3 R0), $.220$ (VC3 R1) and $.011$ (VC3 R2), against adjudicator agreement of $.874$, $.649$, $1.000$, $.631$ and $.735$ respectively (Table A2). The largest is $.250$ against a gate of $.80$. No document is close to the gate, and the gate does not move.

**This realizes C4's second negative-scope condition rather than avoiding it.** The declared consequence is that where the unitizer's own error is large, the operators inherit it identically and the measured agreement describes the unitizer rather than the operators. VC3 R0 is the cleanest evidence: there the two adjudicators agreed *perfectly* with each other ($\kappa = 1.000$) while the segmenter still diverged from them ($\kappa = .250$), which places the failure in the segmenter rather than in the adjudication that judged it. **One rival reading of that is not excluded and is stated rather than left implicit.** The adjudicators are machines (A1), and two models from different families are an *operational* separation and not a statistical one — the same assumption AA3 makes explicit for the operators, now carried by the adjudicators too. Perfect adjudicator agreement is therefore consistent with two instruments sharing a prior and jointly diverging from the segmenter, as well as with the segmenter being wrong. Nothing in this design distinguishes those, so Layer S is read as machine-adjudicated fidelity throughout and the inference is that the segmenter disagrees with the adjudication rather than that it is wrong in some operator-independent sense. Stated at full width and not absorbed: every fixed-inventory quantity in this paper is computed over an inventory whose fidelity to a hand-adjudicated one is poor, and C4-ii says such a quantity is a statement about the unitizer. What survives that condition is the *contrast between conditions*, because the U-free arm imposes no inventory at all and fails in the same way — which is the reading developed below.

**VC2 is M4b's prevalence paradox, arriving where the design said to look for it.** Raw agreement of $.690$ sits against $\kappa = -.005$, because both parties mark about eighty percent of candidate positions as boundaries — marginals $.791$ and $.829$ — so expected agreement is $.691$, a hair above the observed. The coefficient is behaving correctly under the observed-marginal chance model M4b kept rather than replaced, and the marginals are the report. This is precisely the case a prevalence-robust substitute would have flattered into a high number, and the declared refusal of that substitute is vindicated by it rather than embarrassed.

### *The decomposition, and the branch that overturns the predecessor.*

**Edge-level agreement fails the $.71$ absolute gate in all fifteen document-by-condition cells** (Table A1). Between-operator Layer 3 runs $.000$ to $.367$ under U-free, $.227$ to $.488$ under U-det, and $.000$ to $.481$ under U-mod. The maximum observed anywhere is $.488$, against a gate of $.71$: no cell clears it, no cell approaches it, and the shortfall at the best cell is $.222$.

**The ten fixed-inventory cells are the ones that carry the argument.** In U-det and U-mod the units were supplied identically to both operators, so the operators could not disagree about where units begin and end; edge agreement failed there anyway. That is the antecedent of P4 satisfied — units fixed, and the noise floor known rather than assumed (below) — and the consequent follows as pre-committed: **on this corpus, unitization was not where the failure lived, and the predecessor's closing recommendation is overturned by its declared successor.** This branch was named as the likely one before any datum existed, which is why the decomposition and not the verdict is this paper's primary contribution.

**The verdict is stated at the width the design supports, and the difference matters.** What was supplied to both operators was a *shared* inventory; Layer S says it was not a *good* one. The claim this run licenses is therefore that **sharing** a unit inventory does not rescue edge agreement, together with the finding that the failure is not an artifact of **imposing** a cut — because the free arm, which imposes none, fails alike. What it does not license is a claim about a shared inventory of established fidelity: that cell is untested here, because no inventory in this study cleared Layer S. A reader who wants the stronger conclusion — that unitization is irrelevant to edge recovery under any inventory — should treat this run as consistent with it rather than as evidence for it. The predecessor's recommendation is overturned in the form it was made, which was to fix the units the operators disagreed about; it is not thereby shown that units cannot matter.

**The decomposition itself did what P1 claimed it would**, and this is a separate result from the verdict. Over the fixed inventory, selection, conditional typing and edge recovery were each computed as chance-corrected coefficients over a common index set, with no alignment step and no conditioning on a matched subset — so the conditioning the predecessor is criticised for did not reappear, and P1's falsifier did not fire. The three components also separate empirically rather than moving together: on VC3 R2 under U-mod, selection sits at $.220$ while typing over the units both operators selected sits at $.808$, a spread of $.588$ within one cell that no undecomposed figure could exhibit.

**The three conditions fail alike, which settles the substitution objection with data.** The objection to P1 was that fixing the inventory does not show unitization to be separable but removes it, leaving a different task. The U-free arm ran the original unmodified task in-study, on the same documents, with the same operator pair, in the same epoch, and it fails Layer 3 on all five documents exactly as the fixed arms do. Of the three outcomes enumerated in advance, this is the second: both fail in the same way, so the task was not changed enough for the objection to bite. The same comparison discharges L6 in the direction declared for it — if U-det fails and U-free fails alike, the imposed granularity did not produce the failure. **What this does not do is exonerate the segmenter**, whose own fidelity failed on all five documents; it establishes only that the edge-level failure is not an artifact of imposing units, because it is equally present where no units were imposed.

### *The noise floor, and what it removes.*

**Between-operator agreement separates below the within-operator baseline in all fifteen cells.** Layer 3 between-operator values of $.000$ to $.488$ are read against within-operator baselines of $.625$ to $.873$; every difference is positive, with $p < .001$ in thirteen cells and $p = .001$ in the remaining two (VC3 R1 under U-free and VC1 under U-mod), and every percentile bootstrap interval on the difference excludes zero (Table A1). **P2's falsifier fires only when the interval includes zero on every document. It includes zero on none, so the falsifier does not fire.**

**The direction of that result is worth stating carefully, because it is easy to read as reassurance.** The operators are far more consistent with themselves than with each other. That makes the observed disagreement signal rather than run-to-run instability — which *removes* the comfortable explanation for the Layer 3 failure instead of supplying one. Had the floor failed to separate, the edge-level result could have been attributed to instrument noise; it separated, so it cannot.

**A caveat travels with every separation claim, and it points against the design's own interest.** The two operators are not sampled the same way and cannot be (LA4, below): one is called deterministically and returned byte-identical answers across all five repetitions on most cells, contributing a within-operator agreement of $1.000$ by construction rather than by measurement. The declared statistic pools both operators' same-operator pairs, so on those cells the pooled baseline is an average of a measured quantity and a constant, and **a baseline inflated toward $1.000$ makes separation easier to obtain** — biasing the design against P2's falsifier firing. The pooled figure is reported unadjusted because it is the declared statistic, and each operator's own baseline is reported beside it (Table A1 notes). The measured gap is far larger than that inflation can account for, but the caveat is stated wherever the separation is claimed rather than once.

### *The gate verdicts, and the four that pass.*

Across the fifteen decomposition cells and the five Layer S documents, the pre-declared rule yields 64 evaluable gate verdicts, of which **four pass**. Sixteen further verdicts are not evaluable and are reported as such rather than as failures: Layers 1, 2 and 4 are undefined in all five U-free cells, which have no shared inventory, and the Layer 4 ratio is undefined on VC3 R1 under U-mod, where Layer 3 is $.000$.

The four are VC3 R0 under U-mod at Layer 1 ($\kappa = .737$ against $.51$); VC3 R2 under U-mod at Layer 2 ($\kappa = .808$ against $.70$); and VC3 R0's Layer 4 ratio in *both* fixed conditions ($.856$ under U-det and $.989$ under U-mod, against $.70$). **They are not read as a partial success.** All four fall on the two smallest documents and three of the four on VC3 R0, which is the smallest specimen and the one where both operators select nearly the whole inventory — the configuration L4 named in advance as the likeliest way this design fools itself.

**The Layer 1 pass sits in a cell whose observed prevalence index is $.930$, and the paper reports that rather than the coefficient alone.** On VC3 R0 under U-mod both operators select nearly the entire inventory: marginals of $.972$ and $.958$ over 71 units, with raw agreement of $.986$. That is agreement manufactured by construction, arriving in the one place the design returned a favourable selection verdict.

**The verdict nonetheless stands as computed, and the reason is a commitment rather than a preference.** The prevalence floor gates on the base rate *predicted before the run* (M4b), and the predicted index for VC3 R0 was $.108$ — so the document was declared gating in advance, and a prediction is not revised in the light of the observation it failed to anticipate. **The deposited wording admits a second reading, and it is named here rather than left for a reader to find.** M4b says the prevalence index is reported beside every coefficient and that *where it exceeds $.85$* the coefficient does not gate, which reads on the observed index; it then declares that a document whose *predicted* index already breaches $.85$ is non-gating in advance. The analysis implements the second. Under the first, this cell's Layer 1 coefficient would be uninterpretable and the study would carry three passing verdicts rather than four. Both readings are disclosed and neither is applied retroactively, because the implemented reading is also the one that flatters, and a rule re-applied once the results are visible is a researcher degree of freedom whichever direction it moves. **No conclusion in this paper depends on which reading is taken**: that cell's Layer 3 fails under both.

**Observed selection departed sharply from the prediction, in opposite directions by operator, and that gap is itself a finding.** OP_A selected above the predicted base rate on every document and condition — on VC3 R0 the prediction was $.446$ against $.905$ observed under U-det and $.972$ under U-mod; on VC1 the prediction was $.143$ against $.421$ and $.773$ — while OP_B selected far below it on the three long documents ($.092$, $.027$ and $.038$ under U-det on VC1, VC2 and VC3 R1). The prediction was derived from the predecessor's *pooled* node counts and describes neither operator. The floor still gates on the prediction exactly as declared, which is what makes it a pre-registration rather than an escape hatch; the discrepancy is reported as a result rather than used as a reason to re-gate.

### *The asymmetry that dominates the disagreement.*

On the four longer documents the two operators disagree less about *which* units matter than about *how many* do. OP_B returns graphs of roughly 14 to 16 nodes almost regardless of document length or inventory size — 16 of 421 units on VC3 R1 under U-det, 15 of 406 under U-mod — while OP_A returns 50 to 170.

**The alternative explanation was excluded before the number was read as a finding.** A recurring node count of that kind is what a truncated response looks like, and this design's own prompt says a graph that stops early is wrong. Every such call returned a normal stop reason rather than a length stop, used about 1,751 output tokens against a cap of 96,000, parsed completely, and passed the schema validators: the operator is answering fully and briefly. **The asymmetry also replicates the predecessor**, which recorded 14 nodes for the same operator on VC3 R1; this study's U-free arm returns 14. It is therefore a stable property of that operator across two studies and two harnesses rather than an artifact of this one.

**Fixing the inventory does not remove it.** Given 421 units, OP_B still selects 16. That is P4's unfavourable branch in its starkest available form, and it is the clearest statement of why the decomposition rather than the verdict is the contribution: an undecomposed agreement figure would report this as poor agreement, when what it is is a gross and reproducible disagreement about how much of a document is node-worthy at all.

### *The composite beside the decomposition.*

Both members of the published joint-coefficient family were computed in-study on every document and condition, as M5a requires, and are printed beside the layered form (Table A1). **The declared test was whether this design's conditional typing statistic is distinguishable in practice from the categorisation-focused companion, with the pre-committed consequence that if it is not, the decomposition's novelty narrows to the selection stage and the paper says so.**

**It is distinguishable, and the evidence is a crossover rather than a gap.** On VC3 R0 the companion reads $.467$ against Layer 2's $.659$ under U-det, and $.648$ against $.498$ under U-mod: the two statistics reverse their ordering between two conditions of the same document. Statistics that reverse against each other on the same document are not tracking the same quantity, so the honest candidate distinction — that the companion sets position aside as a nuisance whereas this design removes it by construction and then measures selection in its own right — survives contact with the companion's definition.

**The evidential weight of that is stated rather than assumed.** The reversal occurs in two of the ten fixed-inventory cells (VC3 R0 under U-mod and VC1 under U-det); in the other eight Layer 2 reads higher than the companion, so the two statistics mostly order consistently and differ in level. That pattern defeats the declared test, which was whether the conditional typing statistic is *indistinguishable in practice* from the companion — two statistics that reverse anywhere are not interchangeable. It is not the stronger claim that they measure unrelated things. **The pre-committed consequence therefore does not trigger: the decomposition's novelty is not narrowed to the selection stage.** The finding is that the distinction is unrefuted on a within-document reversal, not that it is established beyond it.

**The divergence is reported rather than resolved, and the composite is not promoted for agreeing anywhere.** M4 declared it secondary in inferential status and co-equal in reporting prominence before either was visible, and that declaration is honoured in both directions: no conclusion here rests on the composite, and it is printed for every cell including those where it reads higher than the layered form. Two limits on the comparison are restated rather than assumed away: the composite is computed on repetition 1 of each operator while the layered form averages 25 cross pairs, so the two are not averages over the same thing, and quantities from different inventories are never compared by magnitude (C2).

### *The declared re-analysis of the predecessor.*

Recomputing the predecessor's node layer under a joint unitizing coefficient — charging boundary disagreement rather than excluding it — moves it down by $.409$ to $.765$ wherever a reported comparator exists: from $.663$ to $.136$ on VC1, $.637$ to $.228$ on VC2, $1.000$ to $.235$ on VC3 R0, and $.769$ to $.245$ on VC3 R2 (Table A3). VC3 R1 has no reported comparator in the predecessor's record and is reported as recomputed-only ($.125$).

**The direction was declared before the recomputation and it holds on four of five documents, contradicted on none.** P3's falsifier — that the recomputation leaves the layer materially unchanged — did not fire. The conditioning was doing the work attributed to it. This remains a re-analysis performed by this paper and not a correction to the predecessor's record: its guidelines stay frozen and its published numbers stand as published.

### *Deviations from the deposited design.*

Six departures from the deposited procedure are recorded, each with its date, its reason, and whether it was decided before or after the quantity it touches was observed. **No threshold moved and nothing here is a revision made in the light of a result.** The full statements are published with the record; they are summarized here because a deviation reported only in a supplement is not reported.

**A1 — the Layer S adjudicators are machine, not human.** The deposited M1a structure is preserved exactly — two adjudicators from families used in no other role, working independently under the declared rule, blind to the hypotheses and to which condition the inventory would serve, with a third family resolving — but the adjudicators are models. Human adjudication of five documents twice over plus resolution is days of work by people who must be blind to the study; the alternative was no Layer S, and therefore no operator call, since the ordering is itself pre-registered. **The gate is not adjusted in either direction**, because the direction of the effect is unknown: machine adjudicators may be more rule-following than humans, making the comparison easier, or may share the segmenter's failure modes, inflating agreement for a reason unrelated to the segmenter being right. Adjusting would require a number that does not exist — the move this paper refuses for the Layer 3 document-scale discount. Layer S is therefore reported as *machine-adjudicated* fidelity and every claim resting on it is read at that width. Declared before any call of any kind.

**A2 — Layer S adjudication is elicited in chunks of 25 candidate positions**, each with its own passage and a 240-character overlap into the preceding chunk, rather than one call per document. Every candidate is still decided exactly once, under the same rule, by the same adjudicator. A whole-document call did not survive contact with the providers: one adjudicator spent its entire token budget reasoning and returned empty content, and another answered a 614-candidate document with 64 boundaries. The cost is stated rather than absorbed — a chunked adjudicator sees less context, and a judgement near a chunk edge is made against the preceding chunk's tail rather than the whole preceding argument. The overlap bounds this without removing it, the direction is unknown, and the gate is not adjusted. In place before any adjudication quantity was observed.

**A3 — an unparseable chunk is recorded as unanswered and the pass continues**, a failure mode the deposited design did not anticipate. For positions inside an unanswered chunk the design's two independent adjudicators reduce to one adjudicator plus the resolver, which still decides them, since a position one adjudicator marked and the other did not is by definition disputed. **The degradation is measured rather than described**: 125 of 2,256 candidate positions across the five documents — $5.5\%$ — were decided by one adjudicator plus the resolver, concentrated on VC3 R1 (75 positions, $13.3\%$) and VC2 (50 positions, $6.7\%$), with none on the other three. This bears on one reported quantity and the paper says so: VC3 R1 carries both the largest singly-adjudicated share and the lowest inter-adjudicator agreement ($\kappa = .631$), and the two are not independent, because an unanswered chunk depresses one adjudicator's boundary count in a region where boundaries exist. **The inter-adjudicator figure for VC3 R1 is therefore a floor rather than an estimate**, and the same holds more weakly for VC2. The Layer S coefficient itself is less affected, because the resolver restores the disputed positions before it is computed. In place before any Layer S coefficient was computed.

**A4 — the two operators are not sampled the same way, and cannot be.** M3 requires the $k$ repetitions to run under identical parameters. They are identical *within* each operator, which is what the within-operator baseline requires, but not *between* operators, because this provider pair offers no common setting: one is called at temperature $0$ with the study seed, while the other's family rejects sampling parameters outright and bounds reasoning depth by an effort level instead. Equalising would have meant abandoning the predecessor's operator pair, and comparability with the run whose failure this design decomposes is the reason that pair is used at all. Nothing is done to the declared statistic; what is added is reporting, in the spirit of M4b's requirement that a summary never travel without its marginals. Its consequence for P2 is stated under *The noise floor* above and is not confined to this list. Configuration fixed before any call; consequence discovered during the run and reported rather than repaired.

**A5 — the operator asymmetry was checked against truncation before it was read as a finding**, as reported above.

**A6 — the composite's sampler is seeded, which applies a rule the protocol already declared rather than amending it.** The coefficient's expected disorder is Monte-Carlo estimated and the implementation does not seed that sampler; unseeded, one cell returned $.589$, $.593$, $.594$ and $.597$ across four runs of identical input — a spread invisible in any single report and fatal to a reproduction attempt. The study's declared reproducibility rule already required every permutation and bootstrap stream to be seeded from a stable digest rather than a process hash; the composite was simply not wired to it. It now is, and two full passes over all fifteen cells agree to six decimal places. **This cannot move a verdict**, because M4 declares the composite non-gating. The defect was noticed after the first composite pass had been seen, which is why the direction is stated: the fix was applied to every cell at once, under a rule fixed in advance, on a quantity on which no conclusion rests. The alignment is an exact mixed-integer program solved at a proven $0.0\%$ gap, so an alternative solver returns the same objective; the solver and the seeding flag are recorded in the published table.

## Discussion

### *What this release claims.*

It claims a design, not a finding. Four things are fixed here that cannot be fixed afterwards without ceasing to be fixed at all: the ordering of the three conditions, so that no condition can be promoted after the fact; every threshold together with the reference class it was derived from, so that no gate can be lowered to meet a result; the prevalence floor and the per-document base rates that trigger it, so that a document cannot be excused after its coefficient is seen; and the unfavourable branch of P4, so that the outcome which refutes the predecessor is already written down. The contribution the design offers is the decomposition — three components that the predecessor could only measure confounded — and the reproducibility baseline that makes any of the three interpretable.

**What it does not claim.** It does not claim that extraction is well posed, or that it is not. It does not claim a rate at which extraction fails, a coverage figure, or transfer to documents outside the corpus; C5 states that reading explicitly out of scope. And it does not propose a new operation: the operation is the predecessor's, and the claim under test is the predecessor's own closing recommendation.

### *What the run added.*

The run filled the outcome cells against thresholds that already existed, and both outcomes named in advance as findings rather than nulls resolved in the direction that costs the design something. The within-operator baseline *did* separate from the between-operator quantity, on every document and in every condition, so the disagreement is not instrument noise and the explanation that would have softened the Layer 3 result is unavailable. The conditional typing statistic *is* distinguishable from the published companion coefficient, which preserves the decomposition's novelty beyond the selection stage — established by a crossover on the same document rather than by argument.

**What the run cost the design is stated with what it bought.** Segmenter fidelity failed on all five documents, which is C4's second negative-scope condition realized and not a formality: every fixed-inventory quantity here is computed over an inventory that a hand-adjudicated comparison says is poor. The reading that survives is the contrast between conditions rather than the level of any coefficient, because the free-unitization arm imposes no inventory and fails alike. A successor that wants the levels to mean more than the contrast needs a unitizer that clears Layer S first, and this run says that finding one is the prerequisite rather than a refinement.

**The verdict on P4 is the one the pre-registration was built to make sayable.** Edge-level agreement failed with the units fixed and the noise floor known, so on this corpus unitization was not where the failure lived and the predecessor's closing recommendation is overturned in the form it was made. The width is the one stated in the Results and is not widened here: what the run establishes is that *sharing* an inventory does not rescue edge agreement, on five documents and one operator pair, not that units cannot matter under an inventory that clears Layer S. The decomposition is what makes that statement more than a second failure: it locates the disagreement on the long documents in a gross, reproducible difference between the operators over how much of a document is node-worthy at all — a difference that fixing the inventory does not touch.

### *What this bears on elsewhere.*

One consequence reaches past the predecessor. The framework this arc inherits its layer account from [@zharnikov-2026ao-spec-based-research-post-ai] licenses its central preservation result on an axiom of extraction faithfulness — that when a rendering preserves the locked elements of a spine, extraction recovers them — and names its falsifier as inter-coder agreement on retroactive extractions. That axiom is asserted there rather than measured, and its empirical companion [@zharnikov-2026ap-same-meaning-different-prose] applies it to twin-pair results obtained by exactly the operation this paper measures: recovery from prose with no reference. This run supplies no test of that framework's coefficient or its threshold, uses argumentative and mathematical specimens rather than management-theory spines, and pairs two machine operators rather than a second human coder, so it does not settle the axiom there. What it removes is narrower and firmer: with a within-operator floor now established and the between-operator quantity separating below it in every cell, the disagreement cannot be attributed to instrument noise that a better protocol would absorb. Preservation checking against a supplied reference is a different operation and is untouched — the spine exists first and the question is bounded. The claim here is a pointer, not a result: those papers state what follows for them.

### *What is published.*

Published with this record: the pre-registration as deposited, the propositions and dependency graph behind it, the ontology module for the terms it introduces, and the full statement of every deviation. Published with the run: the extraction harness, the segmentation and adjudication scripts, and every analysis and scoring script, together with the derived tables from which each reported number is rendered — the segmenter-fidelity layer, the predicted base rates, the decomposition, the joint coefficients, the adjudication coverage and the predecessor re-analysis. The code and the machine-readable bundle are in the paper's public repository at [github.com/spectralbranding/meaningfulness-papers](https://github.com/spectralbranding/meaningfulness-papers/tree/main/unitization); the extraction records, the adjudicated inventories, the derived tables and the per-call logs are archived together as a Hugging Face dataset (DOI [10.57967/hf/9911](https://doi.org/10.57967/hf/9911)). The segmenter is a released package pinned by version; the frozen extraction guidelines and the predecessor's run records are available in the predecessor's record [@zharnikov-2026bk-internalization-as-operation].

**Reproduction does not require a provider key.** The repository's orchestrator re-derives the reported numbers from the published records with no network access; re-running the collection itself is a separate flag. The pre-registered ordering is enforced by the orchestrator rather than documented beside it: segmenter fidelity and the predicted base rates are established before any operator call, and there is no path through the script that reverses that. One qualification is stated rather than left to be discovered: the free-condition layer matches nodes by locating each node's span in the document and comparing character offsets, so reproducing those five cells requires the offsets, which are published, together with the specimen texts, which are not redistributed here but are reconstructed by the fetch script from their public sources.

### *What is withheld, and why.*

**The specimen texts are not redistributed, and neither is any record that reproduces them.** The five documents are third-party works. Three classes of run artifact carry their text rather than referring to it, and all three are withheld from every public surface: the free-condition extraction records, in which each node carries a verbatim span of the source; the segmenter and model-produced unit inventories, which are the documents themselves cut into units; and the raw per-call logs, whose prompts embed the passages the operators were shown. What is published in their place is the same material reduced to what the analysis actually consumes — unit indices, boundary positions, and per-call metadata including model version, epoch, parameters, token usage and response digests — which is sufficient to re-derive every coefficient in this paper and insufficient to reconstruct a specimen.

The fixed-condition extraction records are published in full, because an operator's answer under a supplied inventory is a set of unit indices and typed edges between them and carries no source text at all. The specimens themselves are publicly available and the fetch script reconstructs the exact inputs and verifies them against their digests: the matched pair, R0 and R1, in a single document from its producing organization [@openai-2026-planar-point-sets], the independent expert digestions on the author's weblog [@tao-2026-digestion-unit-distance; @tao-2026-digestion-jacobian-counterexample], and the philosophical argument in the journal record [@turing-1950-computing-machinery-intelligence]. No specimen is proprietary and none was obtained under restriction.
